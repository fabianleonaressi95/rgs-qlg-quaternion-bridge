# Q6-to-PRISM periodic-winding obstruction

**Status: PROVED.** This is a no-go theorem for edge-to-edge graph homomorphisms from the Q6 1-skeleton to the PRISM 1-skeleton, followed by passage to the first homology of the full cubical PRISM product.

## 1. Distinguish the two cycle notions

For the Q6 graph,
\[
|V|=64,\quad |E|=192,\quad \beta_1^{\rm graph}=|E|-|V|+1=129.
\]
Its 240 square plaquettes generate the integral graph cycle lattice: in the cubical chain complex of the 6-cube, \(H_1(Q_6;\mathbb Z)=0\), so \(\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2\).

This graph cycle rank is not the first homology of the filled cube. Once the square faces are attached, \(H_1(Q_6;\mathbb Z)=0\).

For the full cubical PRISM product
\[
P=C_{64}\square C_6\square C_6\square C_6\square C_5,
\]
the periodic first homology is
\[
H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5,
\]
with one winding generator per cycle factor. This is distinct from the much larger cycle space of the PRISM 1-skeleton.

## 2. The four-step lemma

Let \(W\) be any closed walk of length four in the PRISM graph. Each step changes exactly one factor coordinate by \(+1\) or \(-1\). Let \(\Delta_i\) be the total signed displacement in factor \(i\).

Closure implies
\[
\Delta_i=m_i n_i,\qquad m_i\in\mathbb Z,
\]
where \((n_1,\ldots,n_5)=(64,6,6,6,5)\). But
\[
|\Delta_i|\le 4<n_i.
\]
Therefore every \(m_i=0\), and the winding vector of \(W\) is zero. Thus every closed four-step walk is null-homologous in the full cubical PRISM.

The shortest possible closed walk with nonzero periodic winding has at least five edges, and five edges suffice to wind once around the \(C_5\) factor.

## 3. No-go theorem

**Theorem.** Let \(f:Q_6^{(1)}\to P^{(1)}\) be any graph homomorphism that sends every source edge to one target edge. Compose its induced map on integral graph cycle lattices with the natural map from target graph cycles to \(H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\). Then
\[
\boxed{
H_1^{\rm graph}(Q_6;\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow H_1(P;\mathbb Z)
\quad\text{is the zero map.}
}
\]

**Proof.** The integral cycle lattice of the Q6 graph is generated by the boundaries of its square plaquettes, since the filled 6-cube has zero first homology. Under \(f\), the four edges around any source square become a closed target walk of length four. By the four-step lemma, that walk has zero winding in every PRISM factor, hence represents zero in \(H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\). The induced map is zero on a generating set, therefore zero on the entire cycle lattice. \(\square\)

No injectivity assumption is needed. In particular, allowing folds or identifying vertices does not evade the obstruction as long as each source edge is still mapped to one target edge.

## 4. Exhaustive finite audit

The accompanying script enumerates all \(10^4=10,000\) formal four-step words in the ten signed factor directions. It finds 270 closed words and zero with nonzero winding. The proof above is general and does not depend on this enumeration; the enumeration is an independent finite sanity check.

## 5. What must change to obtain periodic winding?

At least one modelling assumption must be relaxed:

1. **Subdivide source edges / allow edge paths.** A source edge may map to a multi-edge path. Five steps are the minimum needed to wind once around the shortest target factor \(C_5\). This is necessary, not sufficient: path assignments must still be globally consistent.
2. **Use a source complex with nonzero \(H_1\).** If the 240 plaquettes remain attached as 2-cells, the source cube is simply connected and every induced map on \(H_1\) is zero, regardless of subdivision.
3. **Keep only the Q6 graph cycle space.** This retains the 129-dimensional graph cycle lattice, but a single-edge graph homomorphism still cannot send its square generators to nonzero PRISM winding classes by the theorem above.

The next viable construction is therefore not another vertex relabelling. It is a separately specified path-valued map from a chosen source graph (with an explicit decision about whether plaquettes are filled) into PRISM, followed by an exact winding and consistency audit.
