# Fabian Leo Naressi

**Independent researcher in mathematical physics and computational geometry**  
RGS–PSU–QLG · Quaternionic geometry · Discrete spectral methods · Reproducible scientific computing

I develop and investigate mathematical and computational structures connecting discrete geometry, quaternionic representations, spectral operators and geometric models of spacetime.

My work is organized around the **Realismo Geometrico Strutturale (RGS)** and **Quaternionic Lattice Geometry (QLG)** research programme. The aim is to formulate explicit mathematical constructions, implement them reproducibly, and expose their algebraic and numerical properties to independent verification.

## Research directions

- **Quaternionic and binary polyhedral geometry** — unit quaternions, SU(2)/SO(3), the binary icosahedral group 2I, representations and spherical quotient constructions.
- **Discrete geometry and topology** — Q6 complexes, incidence operators, homology, graph Laplacians and two-sheet constructions.
- **Spectral geometry and discrete operators** — Clifford algebras, Dirac-type and Dirac–Kähler operators, overlap constructions, eigenspectra and symmetry sectors.
- **Geometric field structures** — vierbeins, discrete connections, holonomies, torsion and teleparallel formulations.
- **Multiscale and reproducible computation** — independent numerical audits, deterministic data exchange, convergence diagnostics and verification protocols.

## Research practice

I aim to make the distinction between mathematical derivation, computational evidence and physical interpretation explicit.

Results are classified as:

- **Proved:** supported by a mathematical argument.
- **Numerically verified:** reproduced by specified computations with reported residuals and tolerances.
- **Candidate:** a defined construction that still requires further analysis.
- **Open:** a question for which the required proof or evidence is not yet available.

Finite-dimensional numerical agreement is not, by itself, evidence of a continuum limit or a validated physical theory. Claims about gravity, matter, antimatter or cosmology require separate derivations and empirical tests.
